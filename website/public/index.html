Questions

Is DVR Viewer really free?

Yes. It's free and open source (MIT license). There's no account, no subscription, no ads and no cloud service.

My old DVR's web page asks for an NPAPI or ActiveX plugin. Will this work?

Yes, that's what it was made for. DVR Viewer reads the cameras over RTSP, which almost every DVR and NVR supports, and shows them in a normal web page, so no plugin is needed.

Does my video go to the internet or a cloud server?

No. The app runs on your own computer and talks to the DVR on your home network. Video and passwords never leave your network.

Can I watch on my phone?

Yes. Run DVR Viewer on one computer (or a Raspberry Pi) and open the link it shows on any phone or tablet on the same Wi-Fi. Nothing needs to be installed on the phone.

Which DVR and camera brands work?

Any DVR, NVR or IP camera with RTSP. Built-in presets cover Dahua, Amcrest, Lorex, CP Plus, IMOU, Hikvision, HiLook, Annke, EZVIZ, TP-Link VIGI and Tapo, Reolink, Uniview, Hanwha/Wisenet, Axis, Bosch, Milesight, Foscam and XMEye; other brands can type their RTSP address. Details per brand.

Why does Windows or macOS warn me when I first open it?

The download isn't signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate, so the system asks once. On Windows click More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac open System Settings > Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. The full source code is public on GitHub. Step-by-step help.

Can I watch from outside my home?

DVR Viewer is made for your home network and shouldn't be exposed to the internet directly. To watch from outside, use a VPN to your home network (for example WireGuard or Tailscale) and open the same link.

Does it record?

No, it's a live viewer. Your DVR keeps recording as usual. There's no playback, audio or PTZ control.
