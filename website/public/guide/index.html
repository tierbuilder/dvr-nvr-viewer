DVR Viewer › Setup guide

Watch your DVR cameras in a browser, step by step

About five minutes, no technical knowledge needed. You need a computer on the same network as the DVR, and the username and password you use in the DVR's own app.

1. Download

Get the right file from the download section: Windows 10/11, Mac (Apple silicon or Intel), Linux, or Raspberry Pi. It's a single file with everything inside, so there's nothing else to install.

2. Open it the first time

Because the app isn't signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate, your computer asks you to confirm once. After that it opens normally.

  • Windows: double-click dvr-nvr-viewer-windows-x64.exe. In "Windows protected your PC" click More info, then Run anyway. If the firewall asks, allow it on private networks, so phones can connect.
  • Mac: unzip the download and move DVR Viewer to Applications. Double-click it. When you see "Apple could not verify…", click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to "DVR Viewer was blocked" and click Open Anyway. When asked, Allow it to find devices on your local network.
  • Linux / Raspberry Pi: make the file executable (right-click › Properties › Permissions, or chmod +x dvr-nvr-viewer-linux-*) and run it.

There's no window: the app runs in the background and opens your browser at the setup page. Double-clicking it again just opens the page again.

3. Set up your DVR (four short questions)

  1. Brand

    Pick your DVR's brand. Unbranded DVRs often work as Dahua or XMEye. Not listed? Choose Other and type the RTSP address from its manual.

  2. Address

    The DVR's IP address, e.g. 192.168.1.64. It's in the DVR's menu under Network, or in your router's list of devices. Keep the RTSP port that's filled in.

  3. Login

    The same username and password as the DVR's own app. It's checked once and never retried, so the DVR won't lock you out.

  4. Cameras

    The camera numbers, like 1-8 or 1 2 4 5 6. Click Save and connect and the cameras appear.

4. Watching

  • Switch layouts at the top: grid, 2×2, 3×3, 1+5, 1+7 and 1 (single camera).
  • Tap a camera to make it big; double-tap to show only that camera. ◀ ▶ flip pages or cameras.
  • On a keyboard: arrow keys, L for the next layout, 1–9 for a camera, Esc to go back.
  • Big tiles use the DVR's full-quality stream; small tiles use its lighter stream, which keeps old DVRs fast.

5. Watch on phones and tablets

On the setup page, open Watch on phones, tablets and other computers and copy the watch link. Open it on any device on the same Wi-Fi: it remembers the link, so next time just open the bookmark. Keep the admin link to yourself; it lets someone change the DVR settings.

6. Stopping it

Click the ⚙ button above the cameras, then Stop DVR Viewer at the bottom of the setup page. That closes every camera stream on the DVR.

Always on: Raspberry Pi or an old PC

To watch from phones without keeping your main computer on, run DVR Viewer on a Raspberry Pi 4/5 (2 GB or more) or any old PC. The README has a one-line installer that starts it automatically at boot.

Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do
"Can't reach the DVR"Check the IP address, and that this computer is on the same network as the DVR (same router). On a Mac, allow DVR Viewer under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.
"Nothing answers on port …"The RTSP port is wrong or RTSP is switched off. Use the port from the DVR's Network › RTSP menu (usually 554) and make sure RTSP is enabled.
"The DVR rejected this username or password"Check them in the DVR's own app. If they worked before, the DVR may have locked the account after several wrong tries: wait about 30 minutes or restart the DVR.
A camera stays on "connecting…"That camera number has no camera, or it's offline. Other cameras are unaffected; remove the number on the setup page if it isn't used.
Phones can't open the linkThey must be on the same Wi-Fi. On Windows, allow DVR Viewer on private networks in the firewall.
Picture stuttersTurn off "Full quality for every camera", or use a wired network connection for the computer running DVR Viewer.

Still stuck? The app writes a log to .dvr_nvr_viewer.log in your home folder. Open an issue and include it (remove your IP if you like).
